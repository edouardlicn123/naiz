Audio Path Mechanics

Created: 2026-10-05
Last updated: 2026-10-05

You want to make a BGM track for your game, and that means facing a practical reality: the PC-98 has three parts that can make sound. They are like three unrelated players, each with its own limits — one of them can play only one sound at a time, while the other two can sound simultaneously.

Get these three limits straight, and you know what shape a music asset should take and which path it belongs on. This page covers mechanics only: how the hardware is split up, which music files can be stored, and which conversions are impossible in principle. For which platforms and software can actually produce these files, see mc07 Audio Platforms and Tools; for how to write audio commands in scripts, see sc10 audio commands; for how to register assets, see cf07 Image Registration.

1. The three sound-making parts

First, memorize the division of labor: BGM (background music) goes over FM; sound effects and voice go over PCM.

PartWhat belongs thereWhat it isHow many at onceLimits

The only path in this project that can play music while voice and sound effects also playYM2608, built into the motherboard6 FM + 3 square wave / noiseFour blocks inside: 6-channel 4-operator FM (8 algorithms), 3-channel SSG (YM2149 square wave / noise source), a single-channel 4-bit ADPCM (2–55 kHz), and a 6-channel rhythm section

Two conclusions fall straight out of this table:

  • Background music goes over FM and can sound at the same time as voice and sound effects. They are simply not the same piece of hardware; each goes its own way. That is the fundamental reason background music belongs on FM — not for sound quality, but so that it never has to fight anyone for a channel.
  • Sound effects and voice cannot play back-to-back. There is only one channel: when two commands run back-to-back, the second pushes the first out within 1 millisecond, and the actual sound is too short to hear. Put a page of dialogue between them.

Addendum (only for those who dig into the manuals): the two groups don't overlap on ports either — PCM uses A4xx, FM uses 0188h–018Fh (DIP-selectable 0288h). That is the hardware basis for the first conclusion above.

2. What kinds of music files are there

First, a common misconception to correct: "FM" is not a file format; it is a synthesis method. When people say "make the BGM with FM", they mean "the sound is synthesized by the YM2608 chip" — not some file extension. What can actually be saved to disk and passed between tools is what's in the table below — FM only appears in the "who renders the sound" column.

3. Which music the engine will take

Music can reach the engine by four routes, and the first (A) has been chosen. Two others (B exact playback, C text scores) are kept as fallbacks; the fourth (D) has been ruled out.

OptionRough approachEffortFidelityNotes
A. Teach the engine to read MIDI ✅ chosenThe engine already reads MIDI files; add a translation layer on top: an FM instrument mapping table, how to allocate the 6 channels, and how to convert pitch rangesMediumHighThe same route as the current BGM path; the whole MIDI ecosystem remains usable
B. Play VGM directlyBuild a VGM player into the engine that programs the chip instruction by instructionMediumHighest (exact, instruction-by-instruction reproduction)Must handle its wait commands; useful for exact playback or reference debugging. For producing tools see mc07
C. Teach the engine to read MML / S98
(fallback, not for now)		Write a converter or virtual machine that turns text scores into musicHighMediumUpside: pure text, readable, easy to diff versions; but it has no velocity and no pitch bend, and the ready-made MIDI parser already has both, so not for now
D. Render to WAV on desktop first, deliver via PCMUse an FM renderer on a PC to export the song as WAV and pack it as .pcmLowHigh (offline)❌ Doesn't work: PCM has only one channel; background music would clobber voice and hog the only channel — exactly the problem §1 says to avoid

A is the chosen route, and it can be combined with B: A takes care of reading MIDI, B serves for exact playback and behavioral comparison. D is ruled out no matter how faithful the offline render is, because it fights for the channel.

Why MIDI: the ready-made MIDI reading path already carries velocity, pitch bend, and the sustain pedal; translating these into FM is nearly free (velocity maps directly onto the volume's decay). With a text score (MML) you would have to write a parser from scratch and throw away velocity and pitch bend entirely.

One difference to know: the engine decides automatically — with a MIDI card installed it plays over MIDI; without one, over the FM sound source. So the same bgm command sounds different in timbre for players with the card and without. That is an inherent cost of this route, not a fault.

4. Can WAV be converted to MIDI?

This is the most frequently asked question: you have a WAV (or MP3) in hand — can it be converted straight to MIDI? Yes, and mature tools exist — this route works in principle. For tools, see mc07 §1.4.

But one boundary must be drawn clearly: you can convert to "notes", not to "FM patches". The two are often conflated, and once they are, you end up with the wrong conclusion that "all AI audio is useless".

What you wantWhyVerdict
WAV / audio → MIDI (notes: pitch / duration / stem separation)Pitch detection is a very mature signal-processing problem, and the answer is unique✅ Doable — this is called "automatic music transcription" (AMT; a well-posed problem in mathematical terms)
WAV / audio → FM patchAn FM patch is a set of parameters, while a sound is one waveform. The same waveform can be produced by infinitely many different parameter sets, so no unique answer can be derived backwards❌ Not doable. No reliable lossless back-derivation exists (an "underdetermined inverse problem" in mathematical terms)

Key distinction: notes are the "score"; a patch is the "instrument". Only the latter is that unsolvable problem. So the industry actually works the other way around — compose with an FM patch first, then export to MIDI or another format. For the full workflow, see mc07.

The one exception is academia's DDSP (Apache-2.0), but it works in the other direction: synthesizing sound from parameters, not back-deriving parameters from sound.

So in practice: platforms that only let you download audio are not a dead end — their output can be transcribed into notes. For usable transcription tools, and the "split stems first, then transcribe" order, see mc07 §1.4.

5. Common pitfalls

  • FM has only 6 channels (6-voice polyphony); MIDI has 16. Decide up front which part takes which channel, and which to sacrifice first when things don't fit — otherwise multi-part arrangements will drop notes outright.
  • The rhythm section's sounds are built into the chip — no assets to prepare. The 6-channel percussion reads ROM inside the YM2608 chip; on real hardware you need nothing at all. Only the NP2kai emulator needs the 2608_*.WAV files (the emulator doesn't have that ROM), and NP2kai ships with them; if they're missing, only the rhythm channel goes silent — the FM channels still sound.
    The easier route is to synthesize drums yourself with FM + SSG — the SSG's 3 channels are themselves square-wave and noise sources, plenty for kick drum, hi-hat, and snare (§3 option A's first version takes exactly this route).
  • GM patches ≠ FM patches. General MIDI instrument numbers in MIDI are instrument names like "piano" or "violin", while the YM2608 has only 128 4-operator patches; the same patch with different algorithms and envelopes can sound nothing like the original instrument. Map them one-to-one by name and you get the classic "robot sound".
  • AI-generated MIDI cannot serve as BGM as-is. This is a direct consequence of the previous item: generic GM MIDI must be opened in a tracker to swap patches, rework the arrangement, and drop unusable parts to fit 6 channels. Treat it as a "melody draft", never as a "finished piece". For how to land it, see mc07.
  • Don't let "WAV can't become an FM patch" spill over into "WAV can't become MIDI either". See §4: the former is unsolvable, the latter is solvable.
  • PCM has only one channel. If background music goes over PCM, it will fight sound effects and voice for the lane. This is the fundamental reason for choosing FM over PCM.
  • If an asset's volume never changes, a volume test can't measure anything. If the test samples are synthesized at constant amplitude, an inconclusive volume test cannot be taken to mean the path is faulty. Switch to definite criteria instead, such as whether the path passes sound at all, damping, and decay differences.
