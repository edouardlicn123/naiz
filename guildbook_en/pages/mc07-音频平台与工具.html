Audio Platforms & Tools

Created: 2026-10-05
Last updated: 2026-10-05

The previous page covered the mechanics — what shape the music should take. This page covers reality: which platforms and which software can actually produce something in that shape. Everything listed here has been checked against its actual terms and licenses, including prices and pitfalls.

For hardware and principles, see mc06 Audio Path Mechanics; for how audio commands are written in scripts, see sc10 Audio Commands; for how assets are registered, see cf07 Image Registration.

1. AI Composition Platforms

When picking a platform, only one thing matters: can it give you a MIDI file or a written-out score. Only those can plug into the FM path; the ones that can't — no matter how good the music sounds — can't get in.

PlatformHow to use itNotes
MurekaCommercial service, runs in the cloud, zero load on your machineThe official pages explicitly state that MIDI export is exclusive to Premier members; there is also a pay-per-use API route — $0.2 per track gets you wav + midi, no subscription needed — see §1.1 for details
Magenta StudioOpen source (Apache-2.0), runs locally5 models, each operating on MIDI clips you already have, and the output is still MIDI. Requires Ableton Live 10.1 or later, or its standalone version

The following cannot directly generate MIDI — they only output audio waveforms, no MIDI and no score: Suno, Udio, MusicGen, ACE-Step, YuE, Stable Audio, DiffRhythm, and also Magenta RealTime 2 (frequently mislisted: its official model card labels it text-to-audio, i.e. "text to audio". MIDI is its input — it encodes MIDI into a 128-dimensional numeric vector and feeds that in — not its output. The model file is about 15.6 GB. The project that actually produces MIDI locally is a different one, Magenta Studio, in the table above).

But "cannot use directly" does not mean "completely useless": these tools' audio can be taken and transcribed into notes (see §1.4), and you can even split it into stems first, then transcribe, avoiding the problem of parts masking each other in a mix.

1.1 Mureka: two routes to getting MIDI

Mureka is currently the only commercial platform whose official pages state in writing that "MIDI export" actually exists. Its MIDI is not in the free tier, but there are two routes with very different price tags.

Route A: Premier membership (web)

Quoted from the official subscription page: "With exclusive access to Mureka Studio and MIDI/WAV export, Premier provides the ultimate control and high-fidelity production capabilities required for professional music workflows." From this, the capability boundaries of the three tiers can be confirmed:

TierMIDI / WAV export?What else
Free tier❌ No0 coins
Pro❌ NoHigher-tier models (V9.5 / V9 / V8 / O2) + commercial rights + track extension and editing
Premier✅ Yes (MIDI + WAV)12-stem separation, mixing in any style, any voice as an AI singer

⚠️ What Premier actually costs cannot be found on this page — the prices on the official page are drawn dynamically by page scripts; what you can scrape is only the capability table, no numbers. Rumored figures (e.g. $27 per month) are not officially confirmed; don't treat them as fact. Before paying, open the official subscription page yourself and confirm.

Route B: pay-as-you-go API (recommended if you "just want a few drafts")

The official platform.mureka.ai Export Stem Audio capability (exporting separated stems) turns MIDI into a per-track priced commodity, no subscription required:

ModelWhat you getUnit priceStems
V1wav$0.06 / track5
V3wav, midi$0.2 / track2 (vocals + accompaniment)
V2wav, midi$0.7 / track12 (vocals / bass / drums / guitar / piano / flute / sound effects / strings / synthesizer / keyboard / brass and woodwinds / other)

The official FAQ also confirms: downloading the track itself is free; only stem separation costs extra.

⚠️ Three things to know before using the API (all from the official FAQ): ① the API and website membership are two completely separate accounts with no interchange — credits must be purchased separately; ② credits you top up are valid for 12 months from the day of your most recent top-up; anything unspent expires and is forfeited; ③ no refunds, period — official wording: "all API services are deemed consumed once purchased".

How to choose: just trying out a few BGM drafts → Route B (10 tracks for only $2, no monthly fee, no expiry pressure); doing this long-term and regularly needing 12-stem separation → only then consider Premier.

Understand one thing before using it: its MIDI is essentially "split the stems first, then transcribe", not really writing a score — so its quality ceiling is exactly as high as the transcription tools in §1.4, no better. But V2/V3 hand you the accompaniment as a separate stem, which precisely satisfies the "one instrument at a time" prerequisite. See §1.4 for details.

1.2 Why the free tiers don't help

This is a slightly counter-intuitive but crucial point: Suno and Udio both have $0 free tiers with generous generation quotas, yet the free tiers get you no MIDI — whether you can get it depends on which tier you subscribe to: Suno only with Premier's Studio, while Udio offers it on no tier at all. The blocking mechanisms are completely different, so they must be looked at separately.

PlatformFree tier (official pricing page)Can you get files?For this project
Suno$0, 50 credits per day (about 10 tracks), only the v4.5-all model, no commercial rightsFree tier: audio downloads allowed (with separate download limits), no MIDI⚠️ depends on the tier: Premier's Studio can export MIDI (see below), the free tier cannot
Udio$0, 10 credits per day and 100 per month, full length (2 min 10 s), but only 3 tracks per day❌ downloads have been shut off entirely (see below)❌ no files on any tier, and therefore no MIDI either

Udio downloads are prohibited by the terms of service, not just a disabled feature. Downloads of audio / video / stems were turned off starting 2025-10-29 (the same day as the settlement with Universal Music Group); the terms state plainly that "You may not download copies of any Output", and paid tiers are no exception. A tool that won't give you the files is worth zero for asset production.

So when choosing this kind of tool, "can it give you MIDI" matters far more than "is it cheap" — Suno's MIDI is locked behind Premier, Udio simply has none, and in both cases the free tier's appeal is irrelevant. Suno's Premier itself illustrates the point: the move was not a price cut but a new tier. Conversely, when the free tier gives no MIDI, the §1.4 transcription route still exists — so "audio only" means no direct connection, not no value.

Suno Studio can export MIDI (now officially confirmed) — this previously read "unverified"; it is now confirmed. The official Suno help center describes the export method:

  • First use the Stems panel to split the track into up to 12 independent tracks, then right-click one of them → Get MIDI, costing 10 credits each time; Studio analyzes that track and generates a .mid. The official docs also list uses: re-playing a melody with a different instrument, analyzing chord progressions, re-arranging generated results, and making sample packs together with the audio.
  • The official product page (suno.com/landing/audio-editor) likewise states that stem-level WAV and MIDI can be exported.
  • It is also bidirectional: Studio 2.0 (2026-08-13) added MIDI import, recording, and a piano roll, and MIDI clips can be used as prompts for generation — i.e. besides "exporting", you can also "feed it in and let it continue writing".

But note its nature: Get MIDI is "inferring notes back from audio", not handing you the AI's actual internal composition data. So it belongs to the same class as basic-pitch and Mureka's stem export — the quality ceiling is limited by transcription accuracy (principles in mc06 §4). Single-note melodies and clean bass transcribe accurately; complex chords and vocals will have wrong notes and phantom chords, requiring manual fixes.

Its order of operations naturally fits the workflow: you must split stems first, then Get MIDI one by one, which exactly satisfies the "one instrument at a time" prerequisite (see §1.4).

One unverified lead remains (not confirmed by any official page; don't treat it as a verified option; before using it, check the official terms and export formats yourself): Veena is described by third-party comparison articles as including MIDI even on the free tier. That claim comes from a competitor's own marketing page; whether it's credible is yours to verify.

How to verify: go directly to each vendor's official primary domain for pricing pages and export format details; don't trust comparison articles. One practical lesson: searching for "platform + pricing" turns up a pile of clone sites — lookalike domains reporting mutually contradictory prices, capability tables with checkmarks in empty columns, even invented model names. Example: during this check, a typosquatted site whose domain had one extra "n" claimed "Premier is about $24–30 / month" and listed Studio 2.0 details, looking extremely credible — that price is unverified and is therefore not included in this document. Trust only official primary domains; distrust everything else.

1.3 Open-source models: what it takes to run them

Both NotaGen and MIDI-LLM must run on your own computer, and their GPU VRAM requirements are well above those of an ordinary desktop. Check these three items before running; missing any one makes it all pointless:

ProjectVRAM neededPythonWhat else to install
MIDI-LLM
(an ISMIR 2026 result, based on Llama 3.2 1B)		16 GB or more, requires CUDA 12.x3.11 (conda environment)vllm, anticipation, and the FluidR3 GM soundfont; fast CPU inference also needs AVX512 support

NotaGen
(Central Conservatory of Music)		NotaGen-X 8 GB; NotaGen-large 24 GB3.10 (conda environment)torch==2.3.0 / cuda 11.8, accelerate, optimum

4 GB-class cards (GTX 1650 territory) can't run either one — NotaGen-X needs twice the VRAM, large six times, MIDI-LLM four times. This isn't "a bit slower"; it's a hard failure: the VRAM simply doesn't fit.

One more warning: NotaGen outputs classical scores written in ABC notation (not MIDI), leaning toward "generating score assets" — not the same path as the FM BGM route. MIDI-LLM's example prompts explicitly ask for common ensembles like electric guitar, church organ, and saxophone, while the YM2608 has only 6 channels and 128 4-operator tones; converted across, it usually comes out as "robot music" (see mc06 "GM tones ≠ FM tones").

If you really must run them: ① first confirm 10–20 GB of free disk space (environment plus model files — the disk often becomes the bottleneck before the GPU does); ② use something like Google Colab's free tier to rent a 16 GB GPU by the hour, leaving only the tracker step on your own machine; ③ when disk runs short, use external storage or clean package caches to make room — do not delete this repo's assets to free space (assets/ and disks/ must especially not be touched).

1.4 The transcription route: audio → MIDI

The platforms above are "creation from nothing"; this route is "recovering notes from audio you already have". It has an advantage none of the others have: no platform dependency, no account, no subscription, and any audio can be fed in. For why WAV→MIDI works in principle while "reverse-engineering FM tones from sound" does not, see mc06 §4.

ToolLicenseHow to use / what to watch for
basic-pitch
(Spotify's audio intelligence lab)		Dual-licensed Apache-2.0 or GPL-3.0; just take the Apache-2.0 oneInstall with pip install basic-pitch; CLI usage is basic-pitch <output-dir> <input-audio>. ICASSP 2022 model; supports multiple notes sounding at once, and also pitch bend. TensorFlow is not installed by default — it instead picks a lighter inference library for your system (TensorFlowLite on Linux, ONNX on Windows, CoreML on macOS); if you truly need full TensorFlow, install basic-pitch[tf]
basic-pitch online demoSame as above (free)Runs in the browser — drag audio in and MIDI comes out; nothing to install, no disk space used

The most important thing in this section is order: split stems first, then transcribe — don't feed a full mix straight in.

basic-pitch has a hard limitation, in the official words: "works best with one instrument at a time" — in a full orchestral mix several parts mask each other and the transcription comes out a mess. Meanwhile §1.1's Mureka Export Stem Audio V3 / V2 hands you exactly the pre-split stems (V3 = vocals + accompaniment, V2 = 12 stems), and splitting out the accompaniment before per-stem transcription satisfies this prerequisite precisely. So for the most common situation — "all you have is a multi-instrument mix" — the correct order is:

generate (any tool) → stem split → per-track transcription → MIDI → tracker

This is not an "optional optimization"; it is a prerequisite that decides success or failure. Conversely, humming a single-note melody yourself is still the cleanest approach — the demo page itself says "press record and hum a tune into the microphone". Once the melody is clear in your head, humming it once and converting to MIDI is faster and more accurate than any generative tool.

2.1 Two easy pitfalls

FamiTracker is the wrong tool for this job. Its own site describes it as "a free windows tracker for producing music for the NES/Famicom-systems" (a free Windows tracker for making music for Nintendo's Famicom). The last release is 0.4.6 (2015-02-04), GPL. Two facts are especially easy to mix up:

  • The site does still say "MIDI input devices are supported" — but that means playing live from a connected MIDI keyboard, not importing .mid files;
  • MIDI import was removed back in 0.4.3 (2014-06-07) (changelog wording: "Removed MIDI import"), and it has no MIDI export at all — the only exports are NSF / WAV / text formats.

In other words, if you wanted to use FamiTracker for the "AI-generated MIDI → FM" route, it falls short on both ends: MIDI can't get in, and MIDI can't come out.

FamiStudio doesn't support the YM2612 either. It is often mistaken for "a tracker supporting many sound sources". It only covers the Nintendo side: 2A03, VRC6 / VRC7, FDS, N163, MMC5, 5E01, etc. — no YM2612 / YM2608.

3. Recommended Workflow

① Mureka Premier / Suno Studio / Magenta Studio ──▶ MIDI  ┐
② any tool ──▶ stem split ──▶ per-track transcription ──▶ MIDI   ┤
③ hum it yourself ──▶ per-track transcription ──▶ MIDI          ┘
                                            │
                                            ▼
tracker (BambooTracker / Furnace)           │
├─────▶ libOPNMIDI dumper ──▶ VGM ──▶ OPNplug audition (audition loop)
                                            ▼
└─────▶ engine (YM2608 backend)
Side branch (sound effects / voice):
any WAV ──wav_convert.py──▶ .pcm ──▶ engine (takes the single PCM channel)
  • MIDI is the exchange format; FM is only responsible for producing the sound. This is the sole prerequisite for all the AI tools above to plug in — don't expect AI to hand you FM tones directly; it has no such ability (reasons in mc06 §4).
  • Tones can start from WOPN or community tone banks, but verify each license one by one (see §4).
  • A tracker's GPL covers only the software itself; music data made with it is not infected (see §4).
  • When sound effects and voice take the PCM side branch, there must be one page of dialogue between them (because PCM has only one channel; see mc06 §1).

Where AI fits: it saves the effort of "thinking up a melody", not the effort of "producing a finished track". What AI gives you is generic GM MIDI, while the YM2608 has only 6 channels and 128 4-operator tones; converted directly, it usually comes out as "robot music" (see mc06 "GM tones ≠ FM tones").

So the AI's MIDI must be redone in a tracker: swap in FM tones, adjust the arrangement, and delete the parts you can't use to fit 6 channels. Treat it as a "melody draft", not a "finished piece". If you'd rather skip AI entirely and compose directly in a tracker, that costs nothing and depends on nothing, and the result is often higher quality — AI is not a necessity.

The three entry points, ranked by how much they cost (assuming a local 4 GB GPU without AVX512): ③ zero cost, zero dependencies — once the melody is clear, just hum it; ② nearly zero cost — Mureka API's V3 at $0.2 per track; ① the most expensive or the most hassle — Premier needs a monthly fee, Magenta Studio needs Ableton. Try ③ first; don't subscribe right away.

Also, keep two things distinct: composition AI is "creation from nothing", giving you a melody that never existed before; transcription is "recovering notes from existing audio" — it doesn't save the "thinking up a melody" effort, only the "typing the melody into a tracker" effort (see §1.4).

4. License Notes

  • GPL's "contagion" applies to code, not to the data produced with it. FamiTracker, BambooTracker, Furnace, and mdxtools are all GPL, but music made with them can be shipped in any engine without open-sourcing your engine.
  • "Referencing its code" is a different matter. libOPNMIDI is mainly LGPL-3.0, but it embeds license-incompatible third-party cores (MAME's YM2612 / OPN core, Genesis Plus GX). To reference or port its code, you must check the SPDX license header of every file, not just the top-level LICENSE of the repo.
  • When taking tone parameters from community tone banks, if the bank itself states no license, treat it as "unknown origin" and only use it after verifying.
  • This project's rules are in AGENTS.md §10: GPL v2 code must not be copied directly; copied code must carry the source project + GitHub URL + license comment.
